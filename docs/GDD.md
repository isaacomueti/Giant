# GIANT — Game Design Document

Oct 21, 2026 · @ISAAC OMUETI

## Vision and pitch

GIANT is a deep strategy and life simulation where any player can rise from a Lagos ward councillor to the President who protects Nigeria. The working title comes from Nigeria's nickname, the Giant of Africa.

**One-line pitch:** Stronghold's build-and-defend, Tropico's politics and GTA's living world, set in modern Nigeria and spoken in Nigerian voices.

**Design pillars**

1. **Everything is connected.** A fuel price change moves danfo fares, market prices, approval and crime. No system stands alone.
2. **People, not numbers.** Every statistic has faces behind it: the market woman, the student, the godfather, the loan defaulter. Characters remember what you did.
3. **Power has a price.** Every shortcut (siphoning, a godfather's money, a bought primary) is tempting and can end your career.
4. **Unmistakably Nigerian.** Real places; voices in English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo; Nigerian faces, food, music, slang and WhatsApp culture.
5. **Protect the nation.** In the late game, the question is whether Nigeria stays sovereign against foreign powers that want its resources.

**Tone:** grounded drama with natural Nigerian humour. The situations are serious; the people in them are funny, the way real Lagos is.

**Audience:** Nigerians at home and in the diaspora first, then strategy players worldwide who want a setting they have never played. Phones first, PC (Steam) second.

## Player and career path

You create one character and climb from ward councillor to President; each rung unlocks a bigger map, a bigger budget and bigger enemies.

**Character creation:** name, gender, age, home state and the languages you speak. These decide which communities warm to you first and which voice lines you hear.

**Backgrounds**

| Background | Perk | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tech founder from Yaba | Tech buildings cost less; youth approval starts high | Few political contacts; godfathers distrust you |
| Market trader's child | Market women back you early; food schemes cost less | Little starting money |
| Retired army officer | Security units are stronger and cheaper | Youth and rights groups start sceptical |
| Lawyer | Wins more court cases and election petitions | Grassroots voters see you as elite |
| Faith leader's child | One faith network backs you strongly | Must work harder for trust across faiths |
| Political dynasty heir | Big war chest and a godfather from day one | Owes favours; the press digs deeper |
| Diaspora returnee | Foreign investor contacts; strong on the economy | Weak local networks; "japa returnee" label |

**Career ladder**

1. **Ward councillor:** one ward in a Lagos local government. Learn the loop with a market, a school, a borehole and a few thousand voters.
2. **Local government chairman:** roads, markets, primary healthcare and motor parks across a whole LGA.
3. **Lagos State House of Assembly member:** pass or block laws, approve budgets, build alliances.
4. **Commissioner** (optional): run one ministry, such as Finance or Works, for the governor.
5. **Governor of Lagos:** all 20 local governments, the ports, the airport, the state treasury and security.
6. **Senator or Minister** (optional): national influence before the top job.
7. **President:** all 36 states and the FCT, the military, foreign policy and the national debt.
8. **Legacy:** a second term, a chosen successor or retirement, then a final judgment of your record.

You can fall at any rung: a lost election, impeachment, an anti-graft arrest or exile. Falling doesn't end the game. You can rebuild from the opposition, go into business, or become a godfather who makes other people's careers.

## World and setting

The map uses real Nigerian places; every politician, party, agency, armed group, company and foreign country is invented.

**Lagos (Acts 1 to 3)**

| District | Role in the game |
| --- | --- |
| Lagos Island and Marina | Banks, government offices, big markets; high revenue, chronic traffic |
| Ikoyi, Victoria Island, Lekki | Wealthy residents, finance and tech firms; main tax base; flood risk |
| Yaba | Tech hubs, universities, startups; centre of youth opinion |
| Ikeja | State seat of government, the airport, the electronics market |
| Apapa | Seaports and fuel depots; customs revenue; tanker gridlock |
| Mushin, Oshodi, Agege | Dense working-class areas, motor parks and markets; turf politics |
| Ikorodu, Epe, Badagry | Farmland, fishing, new estates, border trade |

**Nigeria (Acts 4 and 5):** all 36 states and the FCT become playable. Farming belts, the oil and gas of the Niger Delta, trade hubs such as Kano, Onitsha and Aba, and Abuja as the federal seat each have their own economy, languages, leaders and risks. No region or ethnic group is written as the villain.

**Invented institutions** (placeholder names)

**Armed groups and threats** (all invented)

- **Black Harmattan:** insurgents operating from remote border forests.
- **Creek Lords:** oil thieves and pipeline saboteurs in the delta creeks.
- **Forest Riders:** kidnap-for-ransom gangs on highways.
- **The Syndicate:** the Lagos underworld of illegal levies, smuggling and hired election thugs.
- **Gulf pirates:** attackers of tankers and fishing boats offshore.

**Foreign powers and outside players** (invented, with clear real-world parallels)

- **Vestria:** a western superpower. It offers military aid, then wants bases, oil blocks and votes at world bodies. In the late game it runs a campaign to destabilise Nigeria and "stabilise" it on its own terms.
- **Ostrakhan:** an eastern superpower that builds railways and ports fast, with loans that carry collateral clauses.
- **International Monetary Board (IMB):** lends in a crisis, then demands subsidy removal, devaluation and spending cuts.
- **Halvorsen Petroleum:** a multinational oil company with deep pockets and a short memory about spills.
- **West African Union (WAU):** your neighbours, allies or rivals depending on trade and border policy.

## Characters

Characters are the heart of the game: about 40 named characters in the Lagos campaign, each with goals, memory and a voice, plus thousands of generated citizens.

**How a named character works**

- **Traits** from 0 to 100: loyalty, ambition, greed, integrity, faith, influence, temper.
- **Goals** they chase on their own: the godfather wants contracts, the journalist wants a scandal, the student wants a loan.
- **Memory:** every promise kept or broken, every favour, insult and payment is logged, and they bring it up later.
- **Relationships** with you and with each other. Enemies can become allies, and allies can be turned against you.
- **Voice:** a fixed voice, language mix and way of speaking. The Iyaloja speaks Yoruba-laced Pidgin; the professor speaks formal English.

**Generated citizens:** every street is filled with procedural citizens, each with a name, job, income, family, faith, district and approval. The game samples them for news vox pops, WhatsApp forwards and complaints, so policies feel personal.

**AI-driven conversation:** named characters talk through a language model that sees their character bible, their memory and the current game state. The model only writes words; game rules decide outcomes. A character can't hand you ₦1 billion just because the model wrote that line.

## Core loop and building

Every session runs one loop: build, earn, spend, win support, hold power and defend, with each turn funding the next.

```
Each turn of the loop funds the next

        Build (markets, power, schools)
          ↑                         ↓
  Defend (police, army,      Earn (taxes, trade,
          diplomacy)                allocation)
          ↑    [Shortcuts speed up    ↓
          |     any step but raise   |
          |     your heat]           ↓
  Hold power (elections,     Spend (projects,
              alliances)            loans, wages)
          ↖                         ↙
        Win support (approval by group)
```

Shortcuts such as siphoning or a godfather's money make any step faster, but they feed the heat meter described under Money.

**Building categories**

| Category | Examples | Main effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commerce | Markets, plazas, electronics hubs | Trade, jobs, levies |
| Industry | Factories, refineries, cement and assembly plants | Jobs, goods, exports, pollution |
| Agriculture | Farms, fish ponds, poultry, rice mills, cold storage | Food supply and prices |
| Power and water | Gas plants, solar farms, substations, water works, boreholes | Unlocks business; approval |
| Transport | Roads, bridges, BRT, rail, ferries, motor parks, airport, seaport | Moves people and goods; traffic |
| Education | Primary and secondary schools, universities, polytechnics, libraries | Skills, literacy, culture |
| Health | Primary health centres, hospitals | Productivity, lifespan, japa |
| Faith | Churches and mosques, built by communities on land you grant | Community trust, stability |
| Security | Police stations, barracks, naval base, checkpoints | Order and defence |
| Culture | Stadiums, event centres, film studios, parks | Happiness, tourism |
| Housing | Estates, low-cost housing; slums grow where you don't build | Population, rents |
| Tech | Tech hubs, data centres, fibre | High-wage jobs, youth approval |

**Resource chains** (Stronghold's stockpile, the Nigerian way)

- **Food:** farm → truck on the highway → market → household. Bad roads, checkpoints and touts each add cost.
- **Fuel:** refinery or imports → depot → tanker → filling station. Scarcity stops transport and generators.
- **Power:** plant → grid → district. Each district shows hours of supply per day; generators fill the gap at a price.
- **Water:** water works and boreholes → homes and industry.
- **Building materials:** cement, sand and steel set construction speed and cost.
- **Data:** fibre and towers → tech hubs, fintech and online businesses.

As with Stronghold's granary, shortages show up in approval almost at once.

## Money: treasury and personal account

You always see two balances, and the gap between them is the moral core of the game.

| Account | Money in | Money out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treasury (state, later federal) | Taxes, levies, federal allocation, port and customs duties, oil revenue (as President), loans, foreign grants | Salaries, pensions, upkeep, projects, subsidies, debt service, security |
| Personal account | Salary and allowances, investments, your own businesses, campaign donations, "gifts" and kickbacks | Campaigns, lifestyle, family, security detail, legal fees, bribes |

**Siphoning** means moving treasury money to yourself: padded contracts, ghost workers, inflated project costs, unaudited "security votes", diverted grants. Each method has a size, a speed and a trace level. Your own officials siphon too, without telling you, unless you audit them.

**Heat** is a 0 to 100 meter for how close you are to being caught. Siphoning, a lifestyle your salary can't explain, leaks and enemies raise it. Audits, transparency and time lower it; buying silence lowers it now and raises it later.

**What happens as heat rises**

| Heat | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| 30 | Whispers in WhatsApp groups and on blogs |
| 50 | Ngozi Eze starts digging; the FIC opens a file |
| 70 | Frozen accounts, a travel ban, party members defect |
| 90 | Arrest and trial; fight it in court, flee into exile, or turn state witness |

**The clean path** is harder but pays off: money is tighter, but an Integrity reputation unlocks foreign investment, youth trust, cheaper borrowing and the strongest legacy ending.

## Economy

The economy is a simplified simulation with real cause and effect, so players feel how Nigerian economics actually behaves.

**Macro indicators** (state level, then national): growth, inflation, unemployment, naira exchange rate, interest rate, debt-to-GDP, foreign reserves, credit rating, power supply in megawatts, fuel price and a food price index.

**Micro layer**

- **Households** by income band and district, each buying a basket: rice, garri, beans, yam, tomatoes, bread, fuel, transport, rent, school fees and data.
- **Businesses** by type: traders, factories, farms, startups, transport operators. Each pays for power, fuel, rent, levies and bribes, and earns or fails.
- When the basket costs more than incomes allow, approval falls, crime rises and japa speeds up.

**Policy levers**

- Taxes and levies, tariffs and import bans, fuel subsidy, minimum wage, electricity tariffs, land use and housing.
- As President: deficit and borrowing, currency policy, pressure on the central bank (its independence is a trade-off), privatisation.

**Shocks:** oil price crash or boom, flood season, fuel scarcity, currency crisis, global recession, sanctions, an epidemic, a tech boom.

**Advice, not answers:** every policy shows a forecast with honest uncertainty, and your Finance Commissioner explains the trade-off in plain words. Many choices have no good answer, only a choice of who pays.

Example: removing the fuel subsidy frees treasury money but raises transport and food prices for months. Approval drops unless people can see the savings at work, in buses, power or cash transfers.

## Grants, loans and defaulters

You can fund people directly, and how you design each scheme decides whether it grows the economy or drains the treasury.

| Scheme | Who gets it | Starting size (for balancing) | Repayment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Student loans | University and polytechnic students | Tuition plus upkeep | From salary after graduation |
| Youth enterprise grants | Young founders | ₦500k to ₦5m | None; released against milestones |
| Market women micro-loans | Traders, through their market leaders | ₦50k to ₦500k | Weekly or monthly |
| Farmer input loans | Farmers, paid in seed, fertiliser and tractor hire | Per hectare | At harvest |
| Small business loans | Shops, workshops, small factories | ₦5m to ₦100m | Monthly, with interest |
| Hardship support | Widows, people with disabilities, flood victims | Monthly stipend | None |

**Design choices per scheme:** who qualifies, how they apply (in person, through a leader or through an app), whether you check credit, the interest rate and who hands out the money. Routing money through party loyalists is faster, but it leaks and turns the scheme into patronage.

**Defaulters:** each borrower has a hidden reliability score shaped by traits, income and luck; a flood can wipe out a harvest. Defaults appear as a named list you act on:

- **Restructure:** approval up, treasury down.
- **Forgive:** popular, but other borrowers stop paying.
- **Recover through courts or agents:** money back, approval down, bad press.
- **Investigate:** some "defaulters" never existed, a ghost-beneficiary scandal waiting to happen.

**Payoff:** a well-run scheme returns money and grows the tax base two to five in-game years later. Zainab becomes an economist and Ada's startup hires 200 people. A badly run one comes back as debt, scandal and characters who remember you lied.

## Politics and elections

Approval is earned group by group, and elections are won by combining approval, party structure and money.

**Approval by group** (0 to 100 each): youth, students, market women and traders, workers and unions, business owners, Christian community, Muslim community, farmers, security forces, elites and godfathers, diaspora. Regional approval is added at national level.

**Party and structure**

- Join a party or found one, then climb through ward, local and state executives.
- Delegates choose candidates at primaries. Each wants money, a position or a promise, and remembers what you gave.
- A godfather can hand you a ticket, and then he owns a piece of you.
- Defecting is allowed: it costs trust and opens new doors.

**Governing**

- The House of Assembly (40 members in Lagos), and later the National Assembly, vote on your budget and bills. Lobby, trade favours or go over their heads to the people.
- Courts hear election petitions, impeachment and corruption cases.
- Newspapers, bloggers, skit-makers and WhatsApp forwards shape each group's view of you; the forwards move fastest.

**Elections every four in-game years**

- Campaign: rallies, posters, radio, influencers, door-to-door canvassing, WhatsApp groups and a manifesto.
- Election day: turnout, security and collation. Opponents may cheat, and so can you, at a heat cost and the risk of a court reversal.
- Results night: results arrive live, ward by ward, as a tense set piece.

**Protests:** when a group's approval stays very low, it organises. You can negotiate, concede, ignore or send police. Force against peaceful protesters brings national and international fallout, and the game never rewards it in the long run.

## Security, defence and war

Security has two layers: everyday crime and insurgency you manage on the map, and military campaigns you play as Stronghold-style battles.

**Forces:** police, a state neighbourhood corps, army, navy, air force, the SIB, a cyber command and a coast guard. Each needs bases, equipment, pay and morale; unpaid soldiers desert or turn.

**Threat map:** crime and kidnapping hotspots, insurgent zones, pipeline attacks, piracy, smuggling and cyber attacks show on a live map with their causes. Poverty, joblessness and missing services make hotspots grow; schools, jobs and fair policing shrink them over time.

**Battle mode** (the Stronghold inheritance)

- Defend key assets in real-time battles: the Lagos ports, a refinery, the airport, a border town, Abuja.
- Build defences: checkpoints, coastal batteries, air defence, drones, walls around critical sites.
- Command infantry, armoured vehicles, special forces, gunboats, helicopters and drones.
- Victory is more than military: protect civilians and infrastructure, or you win the battle and lose the country.

**Foreign interference** (late game): Vestria escalates in four stages, and you can push back at each one.

1. Economic pressure: loans with strings, sanctions, attacks on the naira, bought politicians.
2. Information war: fake news, bot networks and leaked documents in WhatsApp groups.
3. Proxy conflict: funding armed groups and sabotaging pipelines and power plants.
4. Direct intervention: a naval blockade, a "peacekeeping" landing at Lagos, seized oil fields.

Your defences: alliances in the West African Union and beyond, diverse trade partners, a strong currency, a loyal and fairly treated military, unity across regions and faiths, and a cyber command. A united country with a sound economy is the hardest target, and that is the game's final lesson.

## Society: faith, education and culture

Social buildings rarely make money directly; they decide whether the money you make turns into a stable, loyal society.

**Faith**

- Communities build churches and mosques; you can grant land, protect worship and support their schools and hospitals.
- Faith leaders carry weight with their communities. Favouring one community lowers trust across the board.
- Interfaith tests, such as a disputed land case or a provocative video going viral, reward calm, fair handling with national unity.
- Christian and Muslim readers review all religious content before release.

**Education**

- Primary and secondary schools raise literacy and workforce quality, with a delay of 10 to 15 in-game years. They need teachers, salaries and power.
- Universities and polytechnics produce graduates. Underfund them and the lecturers' union strikes, freezing graduation for months.
- Libraries and authors raise a culture score, slow the spread of fake news, and unlock book launches and literary festivals.

**Health:** primary health centres, general hospitals and teaching hospitals affect productivity, approval and emigration.

**Culture and happiness:** Afrobeats concerts, Detty December, festivals (Eyo, Durbar, Eid, Christmas, New Yam, Osun-Osogbo), Nollywood studios, football pitches and stadiums. They play the role of Stronghold's jester and ale.

**Japa:** when talent sees no future, doctors, engineers and developers emigrate, shown as a live counter. Remittances rise, but services and growth fall. Jobs, power, security and fair pay bring people home.

## The phone: your main interface

An in-game smartphone is the main way you play, because Nigerian life already runs through WhatsApp, bank apps and social media.

| App | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Chats (WhatsApp-style) | Direct messages with every named character; group chats (Party Exco, Ward 7 Youth, Market Women Association, Family); voice notes in Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and English; forwarded broadcasts and leaked screenshots |
| Bank | Treasury and personal account side by side, transfers, loans, and statements investigators can subpoena |
| News | Newspapers, TV and blogs reacting to your decisions |
| Gist (an invented X-style app) | Trends, hashtags, skit-makers, your follower count, viral moments |
| Map | The city or country, your buildings, threats and traffic |
| Calendar | Meetings, rallies, court dates, budget deadlines, festivals |
| Contacts | Everyone you know, your relationship score and what they owe you |

Decisions arrive as messages. The Landlord texts "Come and see me tonight" at 11pm. The Iyaloja sends a voice note about tomato prices. A broadcast with a fake story about you starts spreading, and you have hours to respond.

The phone is also the cheapest part of the game to build and the most distinctive, which is why it ships first.

## Story campaign and missions

The campaign has five acts that follow your career; side missions and dynamic events fill the space between, so no two playthroughs match.

**Acts**

1. **The Ward** (councillor, Ward 7 in Mushin): the tutorial in disguise. Fix a borehole, settle a market dispute, survive your first local election.
2. **The Local Government** (LGA chairman): motor park wars with the Park Owners' Union, your first godfather offer, your first flood.
3. **Eko** (Assembly member, then Governor): run Lagos, with its ports, traffic, the Syndicate, a fuel crisis and a re-election.
4. **The Federation** (Senator or Minister, then President): all of Nigeria, its regional leaders, insurgency, national debt and the IMB.
5. **Sovereignty** (President): Vestria's four-stage campaign, a national defence, and a final judgment of your legacy.

**Sample main missions**

- **Borehole Politics:** finish a borehole before the rains while three factions claim credit.
- **Tomato Wahala:** tomato prices triple. Find the cause (a blocked highway, crop disease or hoarders) before the market women march.
- **The Landlord's Dinner:** take the godfather's ticket, or run without him.
- **Ghost Payroll:** 4,000 workers on the state payroll don't exist. Find out who collects their salaries.
- **Tanker Siege:** tankers block Apapa and the city has three days of fuel left.
- **The Leak:** Ngozi has your bank statement. Deny, confess, discredit her or negotiate.
- **Black Monday:** the naira loses 30% in a week.
- **Gulf Fire:** pirates seize a tanker with a Nigerian crew; plan the rescue.
- **The Landing:** Vestrian "peacekeepers" come ashore at Lagos. Hold the port and win world opinion.

**Side missions:** get Zainab her loan, back Ada's startup, settle a land dispute, calm an interfaith tension, recover a loan from a powerful friend, organise Detty December.

**Dynamic events:** floods, fuel scarcity, strikes, viral scandals, building collapses, disease outbreaks, a Super Eagles win that lifts the national mood, a celebrity wedding that jams Lekki.

**Endings:** several, judged on the Nigeria you leave behind (prosperity, unity, sovereignty), your integrity, and how history and your characters remember you.

## Multiplayer

Multiplayer is a shared Nigeria where each player governs a state, and they compete, cooperate and finally contest the presidency.

| Mode | Players | How it plays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federation season | Up to 37 (36 states and the FCT) | Each player governs a state; trade, alliances, betrayal; a presidential election among players ends the season |
| Election night | 2 to 8 | A short competitive campaign on one map, about an hour |
| Co-op defence | 2 to 4 | Defend Nigeria together against an invasion scenario |
| Senate | Season players | Players who lose state races can serve as senators and vote on federal bills |

**Turn structure:** Federation seasons are asynchronous. One in-game month passes per real day, so a four-year term runs about 48 days. Players act whenever they like, and the server settles each month at a fixed time, Lagos time. This suits phones on patchy data and busy lives.

**Fairness**

- Every state gets balanced trade-offs: Lagos has money but traffic and floods; a farming state has land but thinner revenue; an oil state has revenue but sabotage risk.
- The simulation runs on the server, so nobody can edit their own treasury.
- No pay-to-win: the store sells cosmetics only.
- Player chat is moderated, with bans for harassment, hate speech and ethnic or religious slurs.

## Art, voice and audio direction

The look and sound must be instantly recognisable as Nigerian: no generic African set dressing and no foreign accents.

**Visual style**

- **Ruler mode:** a clean, stylised isometric Lagos that reads at phone size, with yellow danfos, kekes, okadas, BRT buses, market umbrellas, generators, bukas, gated estates, churches, mosques and the Third Mainland Bridge.
- **Characters:** semi-realistic portraits that keep the same face across expressions and outfits: agbada, kaftan, ankara, aso-oke, isiagu, suits, uniforms and school uniforms.
- **Street mode** (later): realistic 3D in Unreal Engine, with MetaHuman characters given Nigerian features and clothing.

**Art pipeline**

- Concept art and portraits from AI image tools (Higgsfield, Kling), using one reference sheet per character so every image of Alhaja is the same woman.
- Buildings and vehicles from AI 3D generation plus phone scans of real objects (a danfo, a keke, a buka, a market stall), rendered at the isometric angle as sprites.
- For 3D clothing, African outfit packs for MetaHuman already exist, such as a 27-outfit pack on Fab. Nigerian-specific outfits will mostly be made in-house.
- The Nigerian asset library built for GIANT can later be sold to other African studios.

**Nigerian voice options** (checked October 2026; licences need legal review before commercial use)

| Tool | Languages | Licence and status | Fit for GIANT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9jaLingo | Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba, Pidgin | Paid API, MIT-licensed SDK; still alpha | 240+ voices and voice cloning; strongest Pidgin option |
| Spitch | Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Nigerian English (Pidgin announced in Jan 2025) | Paid developer API, Lagos company | Production partner for scripted lines |
| YarnGPT2 | Nigerian English, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa | Open source, Apache-2.0; marked experimental; trained on Nigerian films and podcasts | Cheap self-hosted voices for prototypes |
| SoroTTS | Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, Pidgin | Open, but non-commercial (CC-BY-NC-SA) | Prototype only, unless retrained on commercial-safe data |

- Run a bake-off: 10 lines per language per tool, judged by native speakers, before choosing.
- Pre-generate scripted lines and have native speakers check them; voice AI chats on demand and cache the audio.
- For hero characters, license real Nollywood actors or skit-makers under written contracts covering AI voice and likeness, payment and limits.

**Music and sound:** Afrobeats, highlife, fuji, juju, gospel and waka, with talking drum, shekere and ogene. Commission Nigerian producers; use AI music only for prototypes. The city soundscape carries generators, conductors calling "Oshodi! Oshodi!", the call to prayer, church choirs, market haggling and horns.

## Technology and AI architecture

Ruler mode is mostly screens, data and chat, so it should be built on a TypeScript web stack rather than a game engine; Unreal Engine joins only for street mode. This replaces the earlier Godot suggestion.

```
The game server owns the rules; AI supplies only words and voices

Clients:  [Phone app: React web app, Android, iOS] [PC app: Steam desktop build] [Street mode (later): Unreal Engine, MetaHumans]
             | player actions and results                                          | downloads
             v                                                                     v
          [Game server: TypeScript simulation; decides every outcome]     [Asset storage and CDN: art, audio, voice files]
             |                      |                                  |
             v                      v                                  v
          [Supabase: world state,  [Character AI: Claude API, via   [Nigerian voice: 9jaLingo, Spitch
           characters, memories,    the server; writes words,        or similar; lines cached after
           chats, logins]           not outcomes]                    first use]
```

- **Game server:** one deterministic TypeScript simulation runs the economy, politics and events each tick. In multiplayer it runs only on the server; single-player can run the same code on the phone.
- **Character AI:** the model writes dialogue only, and game rules decide what actually happens. Each prompt carries the character bible, recent memories and the relevant game state. A small, fast model handles everyday chat; a stronger one handles key story moments.
- **Database:** Supabase (Postgres) stores world state, characters, memories and chats; Supabase Realtime powers live group chats in multiplayer.
- **Map:** PixiJS draws the isometric map inside the React app.
- **Mobile:** Capacitor wraps the web app for Android and iOS. Target mid-range Android phones and small downloads, streaming art as it is needed.

## Legal, ethics and sensitivity rules

These ten rules protect the game from lawsuits, store bans and backlash, and they protect players' trust.

1. **Real places, invented people.** No real politician, party, agency, armed group, company or country appears by name or likeness.
2. **Consent for likeness.** Any real actor or creator used for a voice or face signs a written licence covering AI use, payment, duration and limits.
3. **No ethnic or religious villains.** Bad actors come from every group; no region, tribe or faith is the enemy.
4. **Sensitivity review.** Christian, Muslim and regional readers review religious and regional content before release.
5. **Corruption is playable, never free.** The game models it honestly, with real costs to the player and to Nigeria.
6. **Non-graphic violence.** Battles and crime are shown at strategy distance, with no gore. Target age rating: around 16+.
7. **Player data.** Comply with Nigeria's Data Protection Act 2023 and app store privacy rules; keep chat logs only as long as moderation and character memory need them.
8. **AI content safety.** Character dialogue is filtered for hate speech, sexual content and claims about real people.
9. **Licences and trademarks.** Check voice model licences, training data and the final title for conflicts before launch.
10. **Fiction disclaimer.** The game states that it is fiction and endorses no real party or candidate.

## Phase 0 decisions

Agreed on Oct 21, 2026 to fill gaps for the Ward 7 prototype. Later phases can revisit them.

**Story set-up**

- You start as the caretaker councillor of Ward 7, drawing a salary from the ward treasury.
- You belong to the Green Eagle Party (GEP). To stand in the ward election at month 12 you need a ticket: take the Landlord's (GEP), or run without him for the People's Voice Movement (PVM) or as an independent.
- Player set-up is just a name and gender, so characters address you correctly. Backgrounds wait for Phase 1.
- The election comes after 12 in-game months, a compression of the four-year cycle for the prototype. Winning or losing ends the prototype with a summary of your record.

**Heat consequences in Ward 7**

| Heat | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 30 | Rumours on Gist and in WhatsApp-style groups; a small approval drop for every group |
| 50 | Ngozi Eze starts digging and the FIC opens a file; she brings it up in chat |
| 70 | Your personal account is frozen (no spending, no siphoning) and elites withdraw support |
| 90 | Arrest. The prototype ends with a "fallen" summary |

**Money rules**

- Building is refused when the treasury can't cover it.
- If recurring costs push the treasury below zero, staff salaries go unpaid, approval drops each month it stays negative, and nothing can be built until it recovers. There is no borrowing in Phase 0.

**Characters and chat**

- Turns are monthly; chats happen between turns. An intent you accept in chat is queued as an action and the sim applies it at the next End Month.
- Routine chat uses Claude Haiku 5.5; key story scenes use Claude Sonnet 5.5.
- Each player gets 30 AI chats a day; after that, characters answer with pre-written lines.

**Technology**

- The simulation runs on the phone and saves locally, so the prototype works offline. Supabase holds only character memories, feedback and playtest logs, with anonymous sign-in so playtesters need no account.
- Approved dependencies for Phase 0: `pixi.js` (map), `@supabase/supabase-js`, `@anthropic-ai/sdk` (in the chat Edge Function only), `@modelcontextprotocol/sdk` (voice server).

**Clarifications**

- Ward 7 uses five approval groups (youth, traders and market women, transport workers, families, elites). Church and mosque land grants are treated identically and raise families' and elites' approval; favouring one community lowers trust with every group.
- The voice bake-off covers YarnGPT, 9jaLingo and Spitch. SoroTTS is left out because its licence is non-commercial.
- Real brands on billboards (see Monetization) conflict with the no-real-brand-UI rule; settle this before Phase 1. Phase 0 shows no real brands.
